Quick start: what you will do

  1. Understand the requirement: constraints, nonfunctional requirements, RTO/RPO, latency, private access, operations, budget.
  2. Compare realistic services: eliminate wrong scope, wrong protocol, wrong SKU, unsupported availability, or excess administration.
  3. Design hands-on: produce a diagram, implementation sketch, ADR and verification plan every week.
  4. Practice deliberately: answer unfamiliar scenarios, write why alternatives are wrong, retest on days 1/3/7.
  5. Verify current facts: reconcile old names/limits against primary Microsoft Learn docs.

The official pass mark is 700 scaled points; this is not evidence that exactly 70% raw accuracy passes. AZ-305 is a required exam for Azure Solutions Architect Expert; the Azure Administrator Associate certification is an additional prerequisite to earning the Expert credential. Personal study cadence and assumptions

This plan assumes eight weeks from Monday 26 October through Sunday 20 December 2026, approximately 10 hours per week: 75 minutes Monday–Friday, 150 minutes Saturday and 90 minutes Sunday. The dates are suggestions, not an exam booking. For an experienced cloud engineer, spend less time on basic provisioning and more time explaining system-level tradeoffs, risk, availability, pricing and governance.

Daily 75-minute session: 15 minutes recall → 30 minutes official docs + comparative notes → 20 minutes scenario practice → 10 minutes error log. Weekend lab: 30 minutes design + 80 minutes implementation/whiteboard + 30 minutes evidence/ADR + 10 minutes teardown plan. Do not incur Azure charges without setting an appropriate subscription budget and deleting resources afterwards.

Architect decision workflow

Diagrams in this study guide are original Microsoft Azure / Microsoft Learn reference diagrams, not diagrams of a deployed tenant. Use the source links under each diagram for the current assumptions and constraints. An internet connection is needed to display official diagrams.

  1. Restate business outcomes (availability, security, budget, data residency, governance).
  2. Identify must-haves versus preferences and quantify RTO/RPO/latency/retention.
  3. Map actors, tenants, subscriptions, regions, networks, data flow and failure domains.
  4. Shortlist two or three supported architectures and compare feature, scale, cost and administrative effort.
  5. Reject any design violating a must-have.
  6. Decide the minimal supported design, with documented assumptions.
  7. Validate SKU limits, retirement state, test plan, rollback and observability.
