Chapter 1 · AZ-305

01 | Architect mindset, governance and identity

Official Azure reference diagrams·Study guide prepared October 2026

Exam mapping: Identity, governance and monitoring (25–30%)

AZ-305 tests your ability to recommend a defensible design under constraints. Begin with users, network boundaries, organizational ownership, risk appetite, regulatory needs and cost. Do not assume that the technically richest SKU is the best. State the simplest design that meets every explicit requirement, then check its limitations.

Requirements to decisions

Translate words such as minimize operations, private access, automatic deletion, least privilege, cross-tenant, automatic recovery and no downtime into capabilities you can test. Make a short requirements matrix before choosing a service. Mark mutually conflicting requirements.

Management hierarchy

Management groups organize policy inheritance across subscriptions. Subscriptions are boundaries for billing, quota and governance; resource groups are deployment and lifecycle containers. Use resource tags for ownership and cost classification, not as a security boundary.

Authorization vs authentication

Microsoft Entra ID establishes identity and supports authentication; Azure RBAC controls authorization to Azure control-plane resources. Data-plane access can use service-specific RBAC and access policies. An Entra directory role is not interchangeable with an Azure resource role.

Identity lifecycle and privileges

Access reviews periodically re-evaluate entitlement; entitlement management packages resources and approval workflows; PIM provides just-in-time privileged access; lifecycle workflows automate joiner/mover/leaver. Select the capability matching the verb in the question.

Conditional access

Conditional Access combines assignment criteria (users, cloud apps, network locations, devices, sign-in risk) with grants (MFA, compliant device, block). Risk-based policies are not direct substitutes for network-location conditions.

Secrets and key management

Managed identities remove application credential maintenance when supported; workload identity federation reduces static secrets in CI/CD; Key Vault controls secrets, keys and certificates, with network restrictions and monitored data-plane access.

Exam traps and design warnings

  • Azure Policy can audit or enforce resource configuration; it does not grant Azure RBAC permissions to users.

  • A Modify or DeployIfNotExists remediation task requires appropriate managed identity permissions.

  • Use Privileged Identity Management for elevation, not for ordinary quarterly membership attestation.

Guided lab: Governed platform blueprint

  1. Draw a tenant with root, platform, landing-zone and sandbox management groups. Document policy inheritance and exceptions.

  2. Implement a sandbox policy initiative with required tags and location controls. Inspect compliance, then create an exemption.

  3. Create a test group and an access-review design; explain how decisions and nonresponse are handled.

  4. Write an ADR: why you chose Conditional Access over Identity Protection for a network-location MFA rule.

Evidence to retain: one diagram, one option-comparison table, a short ADR, a verification screenshot/command result (where actually implemented), and a teardown/cost note.

Tie-in to your question bank

  • Q3: Design the quarterly access review including nonresponse handling.
